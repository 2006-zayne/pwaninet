{% trans "About Spark" %}

Spark is the AI academic assistant built into Vasto. It is designed to help you find academic resources, understand your campus structure, discover peers, and get answers to questions about your studies and the platform. Spark was formerly known as Pwanimate. Spark uses retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to ground its responses in real Vasto documents and data, and can call site-specific tools to provide accurate, cited answers.

What Spark can do

Spark has access to the following capabilities. It uses them when relevant to your question:

  • Document search and analysis — Spark searches the Vasto document repository (past papers, lecture notes, slides, and more) using both semantic and keyword search. It can find relevant passages, explain content, and provide citations with links to the original documents. It only searches documents you are authorised to access based on your academic unit enrolment and document visibility settings.
  • Academic structure lookup — Spark can look up information about faculties, schools, departments, programmes, and academic units (courses) at Kenyan universities. It can tell you which units belong to which programme, which department offers a given programme, and which units you are currently enrolled in.
  • Peer discovery — Spark can help you find other Vasto users who share your skills, interests, academic programme, or collaboration preferences. It respects each user's privacy settings and only suggests peers who have made their profiles visible to you.
  • Group and announcement awareness — Spark can retrieve recent posts and announcements from groups you belong to, helping you stay updated on course notices and group activity.
  • Notification summary — Spark can show you a summary of your recent notifications so you do not have to check separately.
  • Content search — Spark can search posts and documents by text and return relevant results from across the platform.
  • Web search — For questions about current events, recent research, or topics outside Vasto's knowledge base, Spark can search the public web using configured search providers (Tavily, Brave Search, or Serper).
  • Personal reminders — Spark can create in-app reminder notifications for you, such as study deadlines or task reminders.
  • Study mode — Spark includes a dedicated Study Mode that helps you work through topics step by step, with checkpoints and progress tracking.
  • File attachments — You can upload files (images, PDFs, DOCX, PPTX, text) to your Spark conversation. Spark will extract text from these files and use it as context for its responses.

How Spark works

Spark uses a custom orchestration layer that combines several techniques:

  • Intent detection — When you send a message, Spark determines what you are asking about. It distinguishes between casual conversation, general knowledge questions, document-specific queries, questions about Vasto itself, and requests for personalised recommendations.
  • Context engineering — Spark assembles a bounded context package that includes relevant document chunks, your academic profile (programme, level, enrolled units), and any tools it decides to call. This context is carefully limited to what is relevant and what you are authorised to see.
  • Tool calling — For queries that require specific data (such as a document's metadata, a peer's profile, or your notifications), Spark calls deterministic tools that query the Vasto database directly. This avoids the need for the LLM to guess or hallucinate information.
  • AI generation — The final response is generated by a large language model (LLM) from a configured provider. Spark supports multiple providers (Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter) and automatically falls back if the primary provider is unavailable or rate-limited.
  • Citations — When Spark uses information from specific documents, it includes citations that link back to the source. This lets you verify the information and explore the full document.

What Spark does not do

  • Spark does not access your private messages, blocked users' content, or any documents you are not authorised to view.
  • Spark does not share your personal instructions, preference settings, or conversation history with other users.
  • Spark does not make academic decisions, submit assignments, or communicate with faculty on your behalf.
  • Spark is an advisory tool and study companion. It does not replace official university communication, faculty instruction, or academic advising.
  • Spark does not proactively discuss Vasto as a platform unless you ask about it. It focuses on helping you with your academic work.

Data and privacy

Spark processes your chat messages and any files you upload to generate responses. When you use Spark:

  • Your messages are sent to the configured AI provider (Gemini, Groq, or OpenRouter) for processing. The provider processes the data solely to generate a response.
  • Retrieved document chunks and site data are included in the AI provider's request context. This data comes from documents you are already authorised to access on Vasto.
  • Your conversation history is stored in the Vasto database and is private to your account.
  • Your personal instructions and preference settings are never sent to other users or shared publicly.

For full details on how Vasto handles your data, see the Privacy Policy.

Customisation

You can customise Spark's behaviour in Settings → Personalisation. Options include:

  • Tone — Choose how formal or casual Spark's responses are.
  • Response style — Control the level of detail in responses.
  • Personal instructions — Add custom instructions that Spark follows in every conversation.

Feedback

If Spark gives you an incorrect or unhelpful response, you can report it through the feedback system. Your feedback helps improve the assistant for everyone.
